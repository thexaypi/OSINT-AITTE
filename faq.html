FAQ — Legal & Operational

Answers to the questions clients, counsel and compliance officers most often ask before commissioning work.

Legal

Is open-source intelligence legal?

Collecting publicly available information can be lawful, but public availability alone does not make every use lawful. Personal data found in public sources is still protected by the GDPR and the Dutch AVG. That is why every engagement has a documented purpose, legal basis and proportionality assessment before collection starts.

What legal basis do you rely on?

Usually legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, supported by a recorded balancing test showing that the interest is real, the processing is necessary, and it does not override the rights of the people concerned. Where another basis applies, the engagement letter says so.

Is OSINT AITTE a private investigation agency?

Certain investigations into persons carried out for third parties require a licence under the Dutch Wpbr (Wet particuliere beveiligingsorganisaties en recherchebureaus). We scope engagements to stay within the permissions we actually hold. Requests that would require a licence we do not hold are declined or referred to a licensed provider.

Can you access the Dutch UBO register?

Not as a member of the public. Since the Court of Justice of the EU ruled in November 2022 that general public access to UBO registers was not compatible with EU fundamental rights, access in the Netherlands is restricted. We never claim access beyond our lawful entitlement. If you are an obliged institution with your own access, you may supply extracts for analysis.

Does a sanctions or PEP hit mean someone is a criminal?

No. A sanctions match is a statement about a list on a specific date, and PEP status reflects a public function, not wrongdoing. Our reports never present screening results as legal determinations or proof of criminality.

Do you process criminal or sensitive data?

Special-category data, such as health, religion or political opinions, is not collected. Data relating to criminal convictions and offences is processed only where a statutory ground under the UAVG applies; otherwise it is excluded or referenced at the level of the public source only.

Can people find out you researched them?

People have rights under the GDPR, including the right of access and the right to object. Our Privacy Policy explains how individuals can exercise these rights. Requests are assessed under the law, including any applicable exemptions, and are never ignored.

Operational

How long does an engagement take?

It depends on scope and jurisdictions. A single-entity due diligence profile is typically far quicker than a multi-jurisdiction ownership map. The delivery date is agreed in the engagement letter before work starts.

Do you offer continuous monitoring?

No continuous or automated real-time monitoring is offered. Brand Protection & Intellectual Property Exposure Monitoring is delivered as scheduled review cycles, each a defined, dated search. Other services can be repeated as new engagements.

What do I receive?

A written report that separates collected information, verified facts, analytical assessments and recommendations, together with a source register. Some services include diagrams, matrices or comparison tables, as described on each service page.

Can I share the report?

The report is licensed for the purpose and recipients named in the engagement letter. Sharing beyond that requires our written agreement, partly because reports contain personal data that must stay within the agreed purpose.

Will you contact the company or people concerned?

No. We do not interview subjects, make pretext calls or interact with them online. If direct questions are useful, the report suggests what you could ask yourself.

Can you guarantee a complete picture?

No honest provider can. Public sources have gaps, some jurisdictions disclose little, and records may be outdated. Our reports state what could not be established so you can weigh it.

Have a question not answered here?

Ask us directly. We would rather clarify scope before an engagement than after it.
