Methodology

The ten-stage Intelligence Methodology Framework describes how every OSINT AITTE engagement is scoped, collected, verified, assessed and closed. It applies to all ten services, and it is not itself a service.

Two different lists. The service catalogue lists ten commercial services: what is examined. The framework below lists ten methodology stages: how every service is delivered. Stage numbers and service numbers are unrelated.

The ten-stage Intelligence Methodology Framework

  1. 01

    Purpose & Question Definition

    The engagement starts with the decision the client faces, not with a subject to research. Together we define the specific questions, the entities in scope, the persons who may be relevant, and what a useful answer looks like. Questions that cannot be tied to a legitimate business decision are not accepted.

  2. 02

    Lawfulness & Proportionality Review

    Before collection, the legal basis is documented and a legitimate interests balancing test is recorded. We check whether the work would require a licence under the Wpbr, whether special-category or criminal-offence data could be involved, whether a data protection impact assessment is needed, and whether a narrower scope would answer the question. If the review fails, the engagement does not proceed.

  3. 03

    Collection Planning

    A source plan lists the registers, publications and other lawful sources to be consulted per question and jurisdiction, the languages involved, any costs for official extracts, and the sources that are explicitly excluded. The plan prevents both gaps and over-collection.

  4. 04

    Lawful Source Collection

    Information is collected only from sources lawfully accessible to OSINT AITTE, without false identities, without circumventing access controls and without interacting with subjects. Each item is captured with its origin, URL or reference, and access date, so it can be re-checked.

  5. 05

    Source Evaluation

    Each source is graded for reliability and each item of information for credibility, using the grading scheme set out below. A well-maintained official register and an anonymous forum post are never given equal weight.

  6. 06

    Corroboration & Verification

    A finding is classified as a verified fact only when it is confirmed by an authoritative source for that type of fact, or by independent sources that agree. Claims that cannot be corroborated remain attributed to their source.

  7. 07

    Entity Resolution & Link Analysis

    People and entities are matched on identifiers such as registration numbers, dates and addresses, not on names alone. Relationships are mapped, and every link carries its own classification so that diagrams never overstate certainty.

  8. 08

    Contextual Analysis & Assessment

    Findings are interpreted against the client's question, the sector and the jurisdiction. Competing explanations are considered explicitly, and each assessment receives a confidence level with the reasoning behind it.

  9. 09

    Quality Review & Bias Control

    Before release, the report is checked against a structured review list: is every statement sourced, is every classification justified, are alternative explanations addressed, is any personal data unnecessary for the question, and is the language free of overstatement.

  10. 10

    Reporting, Delivery & Retention

    The report separates collected information, verified facts, analytical assessments and recommendations. It is delivered securely to the agreed recipients, and working files are retained and then deleted in line with the retention terms of the engagement.

Grading scheme

Sources and information are graded separately, following the widely used two-part scheme for intelligence evaluation (often called the Admiralty or NATO system). A reliable source can still report something it got wrong; an unknown source can report something true.

Source reliability
GradeMeaningExample
ACompletely reliableAuthoritative for the fact in question, e.g. a statutory register for registration data.
BUsually reliableEstablished source with a strong record, minor doubts.
CFairly reliableSome doubt about authenticity, reliability or competence.
DNot usually reliableSignificant doubt; used only with corroboration.
EUnreliableHistory of invalid information.
FCannot be judgedInsufficient basis to evaluate the source.
Information credibility
GradeMeaningDescription
1ConfirmedConfirmed by other independent sources.
2Probably trueNot confirmed, but logical and consistent with other information.
3Possibly trueNot confirmed, reasonably logical, some consistency.
4DoubtfulNot confirmed, possible but not logical, no other information.
5ImprobableNot confirmed, contradicted by other information.
6Cannot be judgedNo basis to evaluate the information.

Classification of findings

Verified fact
Confirmed by an authoritative source for that type of fact, or by independent sources that agree.
Source-attributed
Presented as the claim of a named source. Allegations always fall here unless confirmed by an authority.
Analytical inference
A judgement drawn from facts and claims, stated with its reasoning and alternatives.
Unresolved
A question that lawful sources could not answer. Gaps are reported, never filled with speculation.

Confidence levels for assessments

High
Based on reliable, corroborated information with few gaps; alternative explanations are unlikely.
Moderate
Credible information with some gaps or limited corroboration; plausible alternatives remain.
Low
Fragmentary or weakly sourced information; the assessment is tentative and should not alone drive a decision.

From information to recommendation

  1. Collected information

    Everything a source says, recorded with its origin and access date. Collected information is raw material, not a finding.

  2. Verified facts

    Information that has passed corroboration at stage six. Facts are stated plainly and sourced.

  3. Analytical assessments

    Interpretations of what verified facts and attributed claims together suggest, each with a confidence level and the reasoning behind it.

  4. Recommendations

    Options for the client's decision, such as follow-up questions, contract conditions or further checks. Recommendations are advice, not findings, and they are never legal determinations.
